How SEVA.GIS works

SEVA.GIS shows your farm the way a satellite sees it. You draw your field, and the app fetches fresh Sentinel-2 imagery and explains what it means for your crop. Everything runs in your browser, and your boundaries stay on your device.

Step 1. Mark your farm

Draw the boundary on the map, walk the edge with your phone's GPS, or type corner coordinates. You can also upload GeoJSON, KML, GPX, WKT, CSV or a zipped Shapefile. The app calculates the area in hectares and acres.

Step 2. Get a fresh, clear satellite image

SEVA.GIS searches the Sentinel-2 archive on Microsoft Planetary Computer for the newest scene with under 30% cloud. Clouds, shadows and haze are masked out, and the image is clipped to your field.

Step 3. Read the crop health indices

IndexWhat it tells you
NDVIHow green and vigorous the crop is
NDMIWater inside the leaves, an early sign of irrigation need
NDWIStanding water or waterlogged patches
NDREChlorophyll and nitrogen in dense, mature crops
EVICanopy structure, corrected for haze and soil
BSIBare soil, useful for fallow and early growth

Each number comes with a colour scale, a status such as Healthy, Moderate or Stressed, and a plain-language explanation.

Step 4. Check terrain, water and weather

The app shows slope, aspect and a wetness index from 30 m elevation data, nearby wells and canals from OpenStreetMap, and a 7-day forecast with evapotranspiration to help you decide whether to irrigate.

Step 5. Plan the work

Create tractor driving paths for your implement width, three-zone fertilizer prescriptions, travel-time maps for tractors and trucks, and a comparison of two dates to spot early crop decline.

Step 6. Download your report

Export a printable PDF or HTML report with your boundary, satellite image, north arrow, scale bar and index scores.

A satellite shows where a crop is weaker, not why. Use the maps to decide where to walk and check.
