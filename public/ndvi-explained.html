NDVI explained

NDVI stands for Normalized Difference Vegetation Index. It measures how much red and near-infrared light plants reflect. Healthy leaves absorb red light and reflect near-infrared light strongly, so a vigorous crop gives a high NDVI.

What the values usually mean

NDVITypical meaning
Below 0.2Bare soil, water, or very poor cover
0.2 to 0.4Young crop or sparse, stressed cover
0.4 to 0.6Moderate growth
Above 0.6Dense, healthy canopy

These ranges are a guide only. A healthy crop's NDVI changes with crop type and growth stage, so compare a field with itself over time and with the neighbouring plots.

Reading the map

Green and dark areas are more vigorous, and yellow or red patches are weaker. Look for patterns. A patch at the field edge may mean poor drainage, a straight stripe may point to uneven fertilizer or seeding, and a sudden drop across the field may mean pests or lack of water.

What to do with a low value

Walk to the weak patch and check the soil moisture, the roots and the leaves. Compare NDVI with NDMI for water stress, NDRE for nitrogen, and the weather forecast before you irrigate or fertilize.

Limits to keep in mind

Sentinel-2 sees the ground at 10 metres per pixel, so very small farms have few pixels. Clouds can delay a fresh image, and NDVI can flatten out in very dense crops, where NDRE is more reliable.
